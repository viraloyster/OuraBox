# The safe space

What the person does on their PC with OuraBox, and why they'd use it as a workspace. It's the **first priority** of stage 1; AI is the second (see [Plan](08-plan.md)).

Legend: **Decided** means agreed; **Proposed** means recommended but still to be confirmed.

## The underlying question

There are already many places to write ideas (Notion, Obsidian, Google Docs, the system's notes app, a notebook), and several are free. If OuraBox is "another notes app", no one changes their habits. The safe space has to be **very useful on its own**, without AI, without community and without companies.

## What fails today in tools for ideas

1. **Saving isn't moving forward.** Ideas get written down and are never revisited.
2. **The blank page overwhelms.** The most powerful tools are infinite canvases that paralyze most people.
3. **They don't tell the why from the how.** When the "how" fails, the whole idea is thrown away, including the "why", which may have been worth it.
4. **They don't help you see the assumptions** (the box).
5. **They don't feel like a safe place** to write your wildest or most personal ideas.

## What the safe space does

### Decided

- **Full-size documents, as a starting point:** a minimalist Word with a difference. On the same blank page you can write, draw or paint freely and add images, without worries. Let each person do whatever they want with the blank document.
- **It's built in parts:** it starts with documents, and features are added as the need appears.
- **Up to four documents at once:** you drag a document from the list onto the open page. In each one you can write or draw separately.
- **Pages like Word's, with deep zoom:** the page is letter size by default (or A4, legal, square, 16:9 or custom) and can be zoomed in up to 10,000 %, to work on tiny details on the same page. You write inline or in **free text boxes**, wherever you want.
- **An idea that arrives in the middle of another:** the quick note is sent to the right project without leaving the one you're working on, and waits, marked as pending, until it's opened.
- **The global keyboard shortcut for quick notes is secondary.** It's not a priority for now.
- **Quick notes:** a section to capture ideas in the moment, without friction.
- **Deep ideas:** a section to work on ideas seriously.
- **Projects:** folders in the list on the left. Each project has its **files** (deep ideas) and its **notes** (quick ones), kept apart. Implemented; see below.
- **Draw:** being able to draw to organize ideas, or simply to relax mentally.
- **Everything connects to everything**, but without overwhelming: connections are shown according to what the person needs at each moment. Not built yet.
- **There are never dead ends:** if someone goes blank on a problem, they always have somewhere to go: draw, organize or work on another project.
- **It works for any field**, with code or without it.
- **Private by default.**
- **Occasional notices, never overwhelming** (see [Design](10-design.md)).
- **Customizing the profile:** an optional feature, for later.

### Decided and implemented: the difference

The five features live in the **"About this idea"** panel of each document (the light bulb button), which is **closed by default**: the structure appears when the person invites it, never before. A document with none of this works just the same.

1. **The box: "What are you taking for granted?"** A list of assumptions, each one marked as *I accept it* (a real wall, for now), *I want to break it* (this is where the out-of-the-box work is) or *I don't know* (it needs finding out). No verdicts and no AI: it's the person thinking better.
2. **A single next step.** One, not a list. It appears softly above the page; when marked as done it says goodbye calmly and the panel invites you to choose the next one. No deadlines, no counters.
3. **The why and the attempts.** "What I want to achieve" is the idea's why. "New attempt" opens another path toward the same thing: it keeps the why and the box, and the text starts blank. The previous attempt stays saved, with honor.
4. **The history: "See how it grew".** Each document saves a version every few minutes of work. With a slider you go through the earlier versions (read-only) and can bring any of them back; the current version isn't lost.
5. **Ideas that come back.** At most once a day, when OuraBox opens, a single idea that has been still for at least a week gently appears: *Pick it up*, *Let it rest* (it won't come back for a month) or *Let it go*. Never as a notification.

**Letting go isn't deleting:** ideas you let go move to a folded section at the end of the list ("Let go"), from where they can be recovered.

**Nothing is deleted outright:** deleting a document or a project takes it to the **trash**, from where it can be recovered or deleted forever, with confirmation. Letting go and deleting are different: letting go is releasing an idea without losing it; deleting is removing something that's no longer useful.

### Decided and implemented: projects, files and notes

- **Each project is a folder** in the list on the left. Inside it has two groups: **Files** and **Notes**, each with its own "+". Everything lives in a project: when OuraBox opens with none, the first thing is to start one.
- **A file** is an idea to work on seriously: a wide page and the "About this idea" panel.
- **A note** is something quick: a small card instead of a page, without the panel. If it grows, it becomes a file with one button and keeps everything.
- **Moving a document** to another project: by dragging it onto the project, or with "Move to another project". Moving it doesn't interrupt whoever has it open.
- **A deleted project goes to the trash** with its files and notes, and comes back whole when recovered.
- **Zoom on each page**, from 10 % to 10,000 %, to look up close or see the whole view. It only changes how it looks: what's written and drawn stays the same.

### Decided and implemented: roots

To research in depth, or to build universes for stories and novels, in an orderly way.

- **Go deeper:** on any page, you pick a phrase (a problem, a character, a place…) and tap **"Go deeper"**. The phrase becomes a **root**: a page of its own, beneath the previous one, with its space to write, draw and, if you want, add images, and with its own **assumptions**. From there you can go deeper again, as many times as you want.
- The phrase stays marked in the text; with the cursor over it, **"Go to this root"** appears. In the list, each root hangs from the one it grows from, so you see the path without covering the page.
- **Going down feels like going down:** the current page moves away, the root rises from below and the sky darkens slightly with each level, like going under water. Going back up, the light returns.
- **The roots map** draws the project's tree from top to bottom, with each root's assumptions in view (I accept it, I want to break it, I don't know), to see at a glance what each problem rests on.
- **Neutral words:** "root" and "assumptions" work just as well for a research problem as for a universe: a universe also rests on assumptions ("in this world, magic costs memory").
- In the list, a document with roots unfolds like a folder. Moving a document to another project takes its whole branch; moving a document to the trash takes the roots that grow from it too, and restoring it brings them back.
- **OuraBox doesn't generate questions or ideas.** It's a space to organize and isolate problems; the thinking belongs to the person.

### Proposed

- **Capture with a global keyboard shortcut:** from anywhere on the PC a line opens, you type and that's it. Secondary for now.

## Tools like Word, PowerPoint, Excel, Canva or GitHub

**Decided:** they're **features** OuraBox can have; they're not OuraBox or its main interface. The goal isn't a hyper-complex tool.

**Options** (which one is chosen is pending):

1. Integrate them as an extension.
2. Compete with them. Ruled out in practice: they're giants, and competing with them would overwhelm the project.
3. Partner with them. It requires those companies to have an incentive to join, and that's for the future.

**Proposed, in stages:**

1. Simple blocks of our own for thinking (for example, a simple table inside an idea), plus importing and exporting standard formats.
2. Direct connections with those tools, always as the person's explicit decision, because they mean taking data out of the safe space.
3. Partnerships, when OuraBox has users that make connecting attractive.

## Flagship product

**Decided:** for now no hook or flagship product is defined. If one appears, it'll be considered according to the needs.
