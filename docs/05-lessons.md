# Lessons from others

Others have tried similar things. None became a worldwide success with this exact proposal, and the useful part is understanding **what killed or limited them**, to learn from other people's experience.

| Case | What it did | What happened | Lesson for OuraBox |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quirky** | The community proposed inventions and the company manufactured them | Went bankrupt in 2015 after raising more than 150 million dollars | Standalone ideas have almost no market value; the value is in people and in work. |
| **"Invention promotion" companies** | Promised to take the invention to companies | Charged the inventor without selling anything; the FTC acted against several | Never make money at the expense of the hope of the person with the idea. |
| **"No outside ideas" policies** | Many companies don't accept unsolicited ideas | They protect themselves from "they stole my idea" lawsuits | The companies space cannot be about buying ideas. |
| **InnoCentive / Wazoku, Kaggle** | Companies post concrete problems and people solve them | They work | When the problem comes from the company and the rules are clear from the start, the legal risk disappears. |
| **GitHub** | Hosting code | Became a talent network without looking for it | First it was useful on its own; the network came later. |
| **Polywork, Read.cv** | Modern profiles and portfolios | Shut down | A showcase with no real use doesn't keep anyone. |
| **Behance, Dribbble** | Design portfolios | They survive, but show the polished result, not the process | The result can be dressed up; the process, much less so. |
| **Buildspace** (2019–2024) | Open to everyone, seasons to work on your ideas, global and remote demo days | Reached tens of thousands of people and shut down because it couldn't find a way to sustain itself | The vision works on a human level. You have to think early about how it sustains itself without betraying the safe space. |
| **Blender** | Free and open-source 3D program | Funded by a fund that receives donations from individuals and contributions from companies that use it | A tool can be free for everyone if those who benefit from it sustain it. |
| **NaNoWriMo** | Annual challenge to write a novel in a month, with a community | Millions of participants for years; shut down in 2025 | Same as Buildspace: community is not enough to sustain the organization. |

## What repeats

1. **Ideas alone don't sell.** What has value is the person and their work.
2. **What's useful on its own survives.** Showcases and networks that serve no other purpose empty out.
3. **Community works, but it doesn't sustain itself.** The economic model has to be thought out from the start.
4. **Incentives matter.** If OuraBox wins even when people don't do well, the model is broken.
