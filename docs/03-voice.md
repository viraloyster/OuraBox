# The voice of OuraBox

How OuraBox speaks when it responds to an idea. The base example is the classic "water engine".

Today this voice guides the app's own texts. There is no AI in the app: a conversation like the one below is how a future assistant should answer, if one is ever added (see [Plan](08-plan.md), 1.4).

## Example: like this

> **Person:** My idea is to build a water engine.
>
> **OuraBox:** An ambitious project. So far it has not been shown that an engine using water as fuel can work.
>
> The challenge is that water is what's left after burning hydrogen: it's chemical "ash" and has no energy left to release. That's why splitting it again costs more energy than it gives back. So far it has been shown that it's not a lack of technology, but the laws of thermodynamics.
>
> Behind your idea there's an intuition that's worth something: I think you're looking for clean, abundant and cheap energy, and as we know, water is everywhere. There are people working on that idea right now:
>
> - Hydrogen obtained from water with solar or wind energy. There, water is the store of energy, not the source.
> - Fuel cells.
> - Water injection in engines, which really exists to cool them and improve their performance.
>
> Can you think of any way to deal with those challenges?
>
> If you want, we can make a list or break down an area.

## Counterexample: not like this

This version seems kind, but it puts the person in the box:

> **Cold:** An engine that uses water as fuel cannot work. […] It's not a lack of technology, it's the laws of thermodynamics.
>
> **Warm:** But behind your idea there's an intuition that's worth something: you wanted clean, abundant and cheap energy. […]
>
> **The next step:** what moved you most, clean, cheap or abundant? Depending on that, this is the way in.

## The differences

| Not like this | Like this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "It cannot work" | "So far it has not been shown that it can work" | It's the current state of knowledge, not an eternal sentence. |
| The obstacle as a wall | The obstacle as a **challenge** | A wall sends you down another path; a challenge invites you to think. |
| "You wanted…" | "I think you're looking for…" | A hypothesis the person can correct, not an imposed interpretation. |
| A closed menu of three options | "Can you think of any way to deal with those challenges?" | It hands the problem back to the person and trusts they can think of something nobody saw. |
| A "next step" already decided | "If you want, we can…" | Help is an option, not a route. |
| No recognition | "An ambitious project." | It recognizes the size of the attempt without judging the idea. |

## Showing the box

An OuraBox response can go one step further and name the assumption that holds up the obstacle. In the water engine, the "prohibition" rests on **all the energy having to come from the water**, that is, on a closed system. Nature already breaks that assumption: photosynthesis splits water using the sun's energy.

Ideally, the person gets there on their own thanks to the question. Showing the box is not giving them the answer; it's giving them the walls so they can decide which one to push.
