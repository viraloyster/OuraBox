Out of the Box

Thinking outside the box
starts with seeing it.

A space where ideas, quick or deep, naive or mature, have somewhere to live and grow. Without inflating them, without crushing them.

Read the philosophy Follow the project

People are born naive. The world is hard.

When a naive idea collides with the real world, the blow usually kills two things: the idea, and the courage of the person who had it to have the next one. OuraBox is the gateway for taking that naivety into the real world without the crash.

Naivety is raw material

We are not here to take it away from you. We are here to give it a place to work.

Cold with the idea, warm with the person

The reality about the idea is not sugarcoated. Respect for the person who had it is not negotiable.

We don't give verdicts: we show the box

Everything we know rests on assumptions. We make them visible so you can decide which ones to push.

We hand the question back

Whoever thinks outside the box finds the way out. We keep them company.

Each idea at its own pace

Sparks are kept without judgment. The challenge comes when you ask for it, not before.

No degrees in the way

What matters is not who you are or what you studied, but what you bring and what you work on. The field is secondary.

How OuraBox responds

Neither "What a brilliant idea!" nor "That's impossible." This is the voice OuraBox aims for. There is no AI in the app today: an answer like this one is how an assistant should speak, if one is ever added.

Person

My idea is to build a water-powered engine.

OuraBox

An ambitious project. So far, no one has shown that an engine using water as fuel can work.

The challenge is that water is what is left after burning hydrogen: it is chemical "ash" and has no energy left to release. That's why splitting it again costs more energy than it gives back. So far it has been shown that this is not a lack of technology, but the laws of thermodynamics.

Behind your idea there is an intuition worth keeping: I think you are looking for clean, abundant and cheap energy, and water is everywhere. There are people working on that idea right now:

  • Hydrogen obtained from water with solar or wind power. There, water is where the energy is stored, not its source.
  • Fuel cells.
  • Water injection in engines, which really exists, to cool them and improve their performance.

Can you think of any way to deal with those challenges?

If you like, we can make a list or break down one of the areas.

The box around this idea: the "ban" rests on an assumption, that all the energy has to come from the water. Break that assumption and something nature has been doing for billions of years appears: photosynthesis splits water using the sun's energy.
